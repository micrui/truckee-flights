How the airport got here

A dated record of the airport's founding, funding, growth, and community programs, assembled from district documents, federal records, and local reporting. Quotes are verbatim. Every entry links its source. Entries that reflect the district's mitigation efforts and entries that reflect growth are presented on the same terms.

Founding and funding

Pleistocene
Glaciers grinding the granite and andesite high country fill Martis Valley with outwash and lake sediment, leaving the only broad, flat, buildable ground on this side of the Sierra crest. The nearest comparable flats are the floor of Sierra Valley to the north and the Basin and Range valleys at Reno, Carson, and Minden. The airport sits where the ice left level gravel. USGS, Martis Creek Dam geophysical investigation · Truckee Donner Land Trust
1933
An airstrip is cleared at Truckee, used by Boeing as an emergency landing field on the Trans-Sierra airmail route. District history page
1958
The Truckee Tahoe Airport District, and with it its taxing authority, is created by a vote of the electorate, organized by the Chamber of Commerce, whose members were eager to have a modern airport that would bring tourists to the area. District history page · truckee.com, special districts
1978
Proposition 13 fixes the district's share of the countywide 1% property tax levy. The share becomes automatic: no reauthorization, no sunset, no separate line on a tax bill. It has not been voted on since the district's 1958 formation. District receipts under this allocation reach a forecast $9.4 million in 2024, about 44% of total revenue ($9.2 million audited in 2023). District finance page · budget history

The expansion era

early 2000s
The airport's master plan of this era anticipates growth. As CapRadio put it in 2014: Ten years ago, the Truckee Tahoe Airport District anticipated bigger planes and more of them. It planned to build parallel runways and development around the airport was restricted. CapRadio, June 2014
2004
Noise conflict is already substantial: the district processes 936 noise calls this year and publishes annual noise reports. Following a Town of Truckee legal memo, the board adopts Resolution 2004-03; a 2015 staff memo summarizes it as agreeing to support phasing out Stage I and II business jets and to engage in mitigation efforts when triggers are met, including when the portion of jets exceeds 15% of the total. 2004 Annual Noise Report (archived) · staff memo on Resolution 2004-03
2003–2018
The district invests $17.48 million in non-aviation community projects over fifteen years, per its own itemized accounting: $3M toward the Waddle Ranch open-space purchase (2007) plus $3M in forestry there, $2.77M for the Ponderosa Golf Course (2008), $1.81M for Martis Creek open space (2011), $930K for the community pool (2015), among others. TTAD Investments in the Community, 2003–2018 (district document)

The inflection: 2014–2018

May 2014
Surf Air begins scheduled Pilatus PC-12 membership service between San Carlos and Truckee. Airport general manager Kevin Smith: Any way to improve access to the area is a benefit to the community. Sierra Sun, May 2014
2014
Jet operations reach 15.1% of the total (3,448 of 22,764), crossing the 15% trigger in Resolution 2004-03. The district also purchases the Vector VNOMS noise and operations monitoring system this year. staff memo · district announcement
Jan 2015
A staff memo addresses the crossed trigger: There is no debate that Jet operations in 2014 are at or above 15%. It argues the resolution's thresholds should be read against 2004's higher operations estimates, and that boards and staff have worked over the years on mitigation tactics well beyond the scope of the Resolution. The airfield's fuel and service hours have been reduced since 2004; hangar tenants receive incentives for observing the voluntary night curfew. staff memo on Resolution 2004-03, Jan 28, 2015
Jul 2015
The new Airport Master Plan is finalized around managed growth of aviation facilities. A proposed third runway is eliminated. The plan declines to pursue lower instrument-approach minimums, asks Will aircraft using the airport get any larger; how can this be prevented at TRK?, and targets 25% of property tax revenue to Annoyance Reduction and Community Outreach Projects. Board president John Jones reports jet traffic up 113% from 2009 to 2014, and a survey finding that 88% of respondents want airport growth managed. 2015 Airport Master Plan · Jones budget presentation, Sept 2015
2017
The district opens a control tower (a seasonal trial in summer 2017, operating again from December), contracting Midwest ATC at $860,000 per year of district funds, to enhance safety and mitigate community annoyance. Class D airspace follows in March 2018. AOPA, Dec 2017 · FY2020 budget preface
Nov 2018
The no-marketing posture is codified in the adopted FY2019 budget: it does not market for or encourage increased flight operations but responds to the needs of the region it serves. By FY2021 the wording becomes traditionally does not market. No earlier appearance of this language was found in district records. FY2019 budget · FY2021 budget

The heavy era: 2019–present

2019
Total operations peak at 36,379. They have not returned to this level since. district tracking reports
2020
Total operations fall 8%; operations by jets over 12,500 lbs rise 23.7% (3,432 to 4,246). district annual report
Jul 2021
A Bombardier Challenger 605 business jet inbound from Coeur d’Alene crashes during a circling approach to runway 11; all six aboard are killed. The NTSB attributes the accident to an unstabilized circling approach flown at excessive speed, ending in an aerodynamic stall, with the captain unable to visually identify the runway during the maneuver. NTSB, Aug 2023 · AVweb
2021
Jet operations peak at 5,175 (up 74% from 2013). Noise comments peak at 1,320, from 240 households. The board unanimously amends the district's mission statement, removing the word "aviation." General manager Kevin Smith, on community giving: We're flying airplanes and jets in and out of here and making noise … we feel an obligation to get out there, see what we can do as an airport district to provide benefit in the community at large. district data · Moonshine Ink · Moonshine Ink, "Plane and Clear"
2023
Runway 2/20 is closed June 25 to November 16 and reconstructed; the $6,300,961 FAA award for it is the district's largest federal grant on record, and the audit records $6.45M of project spending in 2023. Each accepted federal grant extends the district's grant-assurance obligations, including nondiscriminatory access for all aeronautical users, for 20 years. FY2023 audit · USASpending · district tracking report · FAA grant assurances
2024
Operations by jets over 50,000 lbs reach 450, the highest on record and up 45% in a year; the category did not exist in district tracking before 2015. Jets draw 44% of noise comments while flying about 15% of operations. The Community Benefit budget line reaches $4.9M, including a pivot of roughly $2M per year to direct wildfire-mitigation funding for local fire agencies. district annual report · 2024 final budget · TTCF
2025
The adopted budget carries a $2.97M Community Benefit line and anticipates $1.475M in new federal grants, including the Apron A2 project (parking circles/ramp in front of Unicom). Budgeted fuel: 500,000 gallons of jet fuel against 100,000 gallons of avgas. The district pledges a $2M match toward the new Truckee library, contingent on private fundraising. 2025 Budget Reference Guide · Sierra Sun

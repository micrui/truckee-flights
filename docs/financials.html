Aviation activity does not cover the airport's operating costs in any recent year. Including depreciation, the 2023 net operating loss was $7.06 million. One fairness note: audited operating expenses include the district's community-benefit spending (roughly $1.5 million in 2023), so a portion of the operating loss is discretionary giving rather than aviation cost. The gap is closed by the property tax allocation, which is an automatic share of the countywide 1% levy under California's post-Proposition 13 allocation formulas. It does not appear as a separate line on a tax bill, is not voted on, and has no sunset. The district's budget summary shows tax receipts of $7.3 million (2020 actual) rising to a $9.4 million forecast for 2024; the audited 2023 figure is $9.2 million.

Sources: FY2023 audit; TTAD historical budget summary; district finance page.

Federal grants and what accepting them means

Federal spending records show 29 FAA grants to the district totaling $29.75 million from 2003 through 2023, an average of about $1.4 million per year, with awards in 19 of the 21 years. The largest recent award is $6.3 million (2023) for the reconstruction of runway 2/20. The district's 2025 budget anticipates a further $300,000 in AIP funding plus $1,175,000 for the Apron A2 project ("parking circles/ramp in front of Unicom," in the budget's words).

Each accepted grant carries the FAA's standard grant assurances for 20 years from acceptance (obligations tied to real property can run longer), including the obligation to make the airport available to all aeronautical users on reasonable terms and without unjust discrimination. These assurances, together with the Airport Noise and Capacity Act of 1990, are what place most access restrictions, including mandatory curfews, outside the district's practical authority. On the current grant history, those obligations extend into the 2040s. The district's audited net position is $70.5 million, with $10.4 million in cash; the management discussion inside its audited statements describes the district as being in excellent financial condition.

Sources: USASpending.gov award records for Truckee-Tahoe Airport District; FAA airport grant assurances; FY2023 audit; 2025 Budget Reference Guide.

The economic impact study answers a different question

The district's 2018 economic impact study estimates what flows into the region because the airport exists (about $73 million per year in activity, on 2017 data). The figures above record who pays for the airport itself. These answer different questions, and a complete public conversation uses both.
